Sources and accuracy

Project boundaries

The APE (blue) and proposed Amtrak facilities (red) are traced from Figures 3a–3e of Amtrak's Section 106 attachment C (JMA, March 26, 2026); the limits of ground disturbance (yellow) from the records review's Attachment A-2, including the separate boxes at Canal St near 24th Pl and 25th St. Those figures are flat images, so the lines are approximate, roughly ±3–5 m. The right-of-way easement (dashed orange) is taken from WSP's site plan South – Proposed, sheet X-G-026 (October 9, 2026), page 4 of the FRA attachment. The sheet was placed by fitting its Proposed Building outline to our own massing-model footprint of the M&I building at the sheet's stated scale (1"=120'); the fit is within about 1 m, but it is a drawing-to-map registration, not a survey. The outline was then moved about 4.5 meters west, because the 1998 and 2003 Cook County aerials show a fence along the lot's west side and cut northwest corner that the drawn west edge and corner missed by that much; the shift is our own adjustment to follow that fence, not something WSP drew. WSP hatches only the west, south and chamfered corner of the easement, so the north side is closed along the existing right-of-way line by us. The 1970, 1980, 1990 and 1995 aerials are Northeastern Illinois Planning Commission photographs (April 9, 1970; May 3, 1980; March 20, 1990; April 14, 1995) scanned and georeferenced by the Chicago Metropolitan Agency for Planning; they are not orthorectified, so tall buildings lean. The 1998 and 2003 aerials are Cook County's orthophotos, loaded live from the county's server. The 2005 and 2007 aerials are USDA NAIP imagery, loaded live from the Illinois State Geological Survey's server. Every aerial after 1963 was lined up against the Armour Square field house (the building's outline on each image, at 33rd St and Shields Ave). The 1970 and 1980 sheets, whose published georeferencing was off by about 33 and 35 meters there, were moved to match, and the 2005 image by about 4 meters; the others already agreed to within a couple of meters. Away from the field house the old sheets can still be off by several meters.

Full-resolution maps

  • Sanborn fire insurance maps of Chicago, vol. 3 (1911) and vol. 4 (1912), University of Illinois Library Digital Collections (public domain). These are the same sheets cited in Amtrak's figures.
  • Van Vechten's 1870 map of Cook and Du Page Counties, Library of Congress Geography and Map Division (no known restrictions), cut from the library's full-resolution scan.
  • Rees & Rucker's 1849 Map of Chicago and Vicinity, scan on Wikimedia Commons (public domain), cut from the full-size file.
  • Warner & Beers' 1875 Map of the City of Chicago, University of Illinois Library Digital Collections, cut from the library's scan.
  • 1938-39 Cook County aerial photographs, Illinois Historical Aerial Photography, Illinois State Geological Survey (image service IL_Cook_1938_39), loaded live.
  • USGS topographic quadrangles for 1889, 1900, 1929, 1953 and 1963 (Chicago 1:62,500 and Englewood 1:24,000), full-resolution scans from USGS topoView (public domain). Each is placed from the sheet's published georeferencing rather than from Amtrak's figures, and the sheet record numbers are given for each period.
  • Robinson's Atlas of the City of Chicago, vol. 2 (E. Robinson, New York, 1886), plates 6, 19, 20, 25 and 26, Newberry Library (open access).

Images load directly from the libraries' image servers, so nothing is copied or stitched. Each sheet is placed with a straight-line (affine) fit to present-day street intersections from Census TIGER street data, and its margins are trimmed. Fit errors are listed for each period. Old maps were hand-drawn, so expect some local error beyond those figures. Plate 6 (north of Cermak) is fitted along a single street and may be off by 10–20 m.

Lower-resolution maps

The 1861 and 1879 maps are cropped from Amtrak's own figures (the records review's figures D-4 and D-6). Each one is placed by matching the boundary line Amtrak drew on it to our traced boundary, so it inherits however well Amtrak's consultants fitted the old map.

Notes

  • Amtrak's records review lists separate 1953 and 1963 Englewood quadrangles, but the two figures it supplies (D-21 and D-22) are the same image, labeled 1963. Matching it against the USGS sheets shows it is the 1953 printing, so the 1953 and 1963 periods here use the real USGS sheets for each year.

Not included yet

  • The 1914 Sanborn sheets (5, 9 and 10) Amtrak used for the two parcels south of Pershing Rd. They appear to be from the volume south of 39th St (likely vol. 13), which the University of Illinois has online only in its 1925 edition.
